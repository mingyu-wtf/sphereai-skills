# Shorts Blueprint

Plan a Short that people watch to the end in 15–60 seconds, and then watch again. The output is a script and a shot list.

## Language
Reply and write all deliverables in the user's language (Korean, English, Japanese, …). Ask once if unclear. If the Short's language differs from the chat language, write narration and captions in the Short's language and the notes in the chat language.

Worked example: `references/example_en.md` ("The 2-minute rule", 30 s).

## 0. Decide first (ask if unknown)
1. One sentence: "What this Short teaches"
2. Length: 15 s / 30 s / 45–60 s (with a single idea, shorter is better)
3. Source: cut from an existing video, or shoot / generate new footage
4. Tone and audience (including register: polite vs. casual speech in Korean or Japanese; casual vs. professional word choice in English)

## 1. Structure (30-second example)
| Section | Time | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0–1.5 s | Throw a question, a result, or a twist, on screen and in the caption at the same time |
| Build | 1.5–22 s | One idea per shot. Shots average 2–4 s |
| Twist / payoff | 22–28 s | The most surprising fact or result |
| Close | 28–30 s | One line that flows naturally back into the first scene (for rewatches) |

- Don't open with a greeting or self-introduction.
- If you need a subscribe ask, put it once, at the very end.

## 2. Shot rules
- One idea per shot. If the line runs long, split the shot.
- At every cut, change at least one of framing, distance, or color so it never feels flat.
- Show the scene before explaining it (result → reason).
- When speeding up narration, go only as far as the words stay clear (start around 1.1x and listen).

## 3. Vertical safe areas
- Platform buttons and the title cover the top and bottom of the screen. Keep key captions and faces in the middle, away from the top and bottom edges.
- Like and comment buttons sit on the right edge. Don't push text against the right side.
- The layout differs between YouTube Shorts, TikTok, and Instagram Reels. Confirm exact margins with a private test upload on each platform you post to.

## 4. Caption rules
- One or two lines at a time. Keep each caption short enough to read in about 1–1.5 seconds; break lines so the eye can follow.
  - For reference, common subtitle style guides cap a full line at about 42 characters in English, 16 in Korean, and 13 in Japanese. Shorts captions should be much shorter than that: a few words per caption.
  - Break at natural phrase boundaries: never split a name, a number and its unit, or (in Korean and Japanese) a word and its particle.
- Keep captions in sync with speech, and leave each on screen as long as it takes to read.
- Give color or size to one emphasized word only.
- Captions use the same words as the voice (do a final check for typos and wrong numbers).

## 5. Keep the meaning intact
When cutting from an existing video, first check that no key information gets cut. Don't cut where trimming the start or end of a line changes its meaning. If you must cut, keep the preceding sentence (the thing a "this/that" refers to, or the source of a quote).

## 6. Output format
```
[One-line summary]
[3 hook options + 1 recommended]
[Shot list]
| # | Time | Visual | Narration | Caption | Sound / SFX |
[Closing line] (does it connect to the first scene?)
[Facts to check] [Platform test-upload checklist]
```

## 7. Checklist
- [ ] Something grabs attention within the first 1.5 seconds
- [ ] Each shot carries one idea
- [ ] Key captions avoid the top, bottom, and right edges
- [ ] The last line connects to the first scene
- [ ] Facts and numbers are checked
