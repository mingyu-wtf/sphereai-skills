# Worked example: "The 2-minute rule" (30 s Short)

Answers from step 0:

1. What it teaches: "If a task takes less than two minutes, do it now instead of writing it down."
2. Length: 30 s
3. Source: new footage (phone camera, desk and kitchen)
4. Tone: quick and friendly, casual English; audience: people who procrastinate on small chores

---

## One-line summary
A 30-second Short showing that tiny tasks pile up into a big mess, and the fix is to finish anything under two minutes on the spot.

## Hook options
- A. **Result**: a messy desk turns clean in a jump cut. Caption: "This took 2 minutes. Total."
- B. **Question**: "Why does your to-do list never get shorter?"
- C. **Twist**: "Writing it down is the reason it never gets done."

**Recommended: C.** It contradicts what viewers believe about to-do lists, and the loop-back line can return to it. Use A's messy desk as the background of the first frame.

## Closing line
"So next time you reach for a sticky note…" flows straight into shot 1, "Writing it down is why it never gets done." Rewatching sounds like one continuous sentence.

## Facts to check
- The "two-minute rule" is usually credited to David Allen's book *Getting Things Done*. Verify before saying a name on screen, or leave the name out.
- "Ten small things, gone before lunch" is an illustration, not a measured claim. Keep it phrased as an example.
