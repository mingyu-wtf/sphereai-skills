# Worked example: "Does phone use at night make it harder to fall asleep?"

> **About this example.** It shows the brief's structure and how grading and "unconfirmed" marks work. The searches were **not actually run** for this file, so every source line is a lead marked **[to verify]**, not a confirmed citation. In a real run, the agent opens each source, checks the date and wording, and only then assigns a grade. Never copy these leads into a video as facts.

## Where sources disagree
- Expected disagreement: lab studies vs. real-life studies on **how large** the effect is. When the sources are opened, write it as "Study A found ○○; study B found ○○" instead of picking one. **[to verify]**
- Night-mode settings: some sources may say they help, others that brightness and content matter more. **[unconfirmed]**

## Unconfirmed / still need to find out
- Any specific number (minutes of delay, percentage change). None may be used until confirmed by two independent sources.
- Whether the effect is different for teenagers and adults.
- Whether audio-only phone use (podcasts with the screen off) has the same effect.

## Method
- Search terms: "screen time before bed sleep onset study", "evening light melatonin review", "e-reader sleep study", "night mode blue light sleep evidence"; plus equivalents in the user's language
- Date range: about 2010 to the present
- Languages and engines: English (Google, Google Scholar); the user's language on the search engine popular in their country
- Excluded: product pages selling blue-light glasses, "top 10" listicles without sources
