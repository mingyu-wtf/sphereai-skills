# Picking moments that work as Shorts

Read the whole transcript first, then mark candidates. A good clip passes all four checks:

1. **Self-contained** - someone who never saw the long video understands it. No "as I said earlier", no unexplained "this/that" in the first sentence.
2. **Hook in the first sentence** - one of: a surprising claim, a question the viewer has, a number, a contrast ("not X, but Y"), a relatable situation ("Ever felt tired after just one hour with friends?").
3. **Payoff before the end** - an answer, a reveal, a punchline, a list that completes, a line worth quoting. End right after it.
4. **One idea** - if a clip needs two ideas to make sense, it is too long; split or skip.

## Score (1-10), keep 7+
| Points | Signal |
|---|---|
| +3 | strong hook sentence that works with no context |
| +2 | concrete example, number, story or metaphor |
| +2 | clear payoff / quotable line at the end |
| +1 | emotional or "that's me" moment |
| +1 | actionable tip the viewer can use today |
| +1 | 25-50 s without filler |
| -3 | needs earlier context, or ends mid-thought |
| -2 | long intro/outro talk ("subscribe", sponsor, greetings) |

## Length
- 20-60 s. 25-50 s is the sweet spot for most talk content.
- `tighten` removes 2-10 % of the time; pick a little longer than the target.

## Variety
- Spread clips across the video; do not take 5 clips from the first 3 minutes.
- Mix types: one surprising fact, one story, one how-to list, one quotable line.

## Typical good spots
- the first time the main promise is explained
- a story told in 3-4 sentences
- "here's what to do" numbered lists
- a strong analogy ("like having 10 TV channels on at once")
- a closing summary line

## Avoid
- the channel intro, sponsor reads, "like and subscribe"
- moments that only make sense with the visuals of a previous scene
- anything where cutting would change the meaning
